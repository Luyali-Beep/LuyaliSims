How to Operate the Simulator

This studio offers two complementary modes designed to bridge circuit construction with theoretical analysis:

1. Schematic Circuit Builder: Select circuit components from the left palette (DC Battery, Resistor, Capacitor, Bulb, Meters, and Switches). Drag elements onto the canvas where they snap firmly to a 20-pixel orthogonal grid. Click on terminal pins to route connecting wires at strict 90-degree angles. Scientific Theory & Working Principles

A capacitor consists of two isolated conductive plates separated by a non-conductive dielectric gap. When connected across a direct-current source of electromotive force (EMF), electrons accumulate on the negative plate while being drawn away from the positive plate. This migration continues until the potential difference across the dielectric matches the terminal potential of the supply.

The physical charge storage capability—its capacitance (C)—depends solely on its physical geometry and dielectric medium. Introducing an insulating material with relative permittivity (dielectric constant κ) polarizes internal bound dipoles. The surface charge of the polarized dielectric creates an opposing internal electric field, reducing the net potential gradient and enabling the capacitor to store substantially greater charge per volt applied.

When placed in series with a resistor (R), the instantaneous current is restricted by Ohm's Law. As the stored charge grows, the opposing voltage across the capacitor (Vc) rises, diminishing the net voltage available across the resistor (VR = V0 - Vc). Consequently, both charging and discharging processes follow an exponential rate governed by the circuit's characteristic time constant (τ = R × C).

Mathematical Foundations & Formulas

The quantitative behavior of electrostatic storage and resistive-capacitive transient decay is governed by the following core relationships:

Capacitance from Geometry: C = κ × ε0 × (A / d)
Permittivity of Free Space: ε0 ≈ 8.854 × 10^-12 F/m
Stored Electrostatic Charge: Q = C × Vc
Circuit Time Constant: τ = R × C
Transient Charging Potential: Vc(t) = V0 × [1 - exp(-t / τ)]
Transient Charging Current: I(t) = (V0 / R) × exp(-t / τ)
Transient Discharging Potential: Vc(t) = V0 × exp(-t / τ)
Stored Electrostatic Energy: U = (1/2) × C × (Vc)^2

When plotting the natural log of voltage versus time during discharging: ln(Vc) = ln(V0) - (1 / τ) × t. This yields a straight line whose negative slope directly equals the inverse time constant (-1 / τ), providing a standard laboratory method for evaluating unknown circuit parameters.

Real-World Engineering & Industrial Applications

Resistive-capacitive networks and variable dielectric capacitors form fundamental building blocks across modern electronics:

1. AC Power Rectification & Smoothing Filters: In mains DC power adapters, bridge rectifiers transform sinusoidal alternating current into pulsating DC. High-capacitance filter capacitors connected across the output absorb charge during peak cycles and discharge during troughs, reducing ripple voltage to power sensitive microcontrollers cleanly.

2. Precision Timing & Waveform Oscillators: The predictable exponential ramp of an RC pair provides deterministic trigger delays in 555-timer circuits, heart pacemakers, pulse-width modulation (PWM) motor controllers, and intermittent windshield wiper triggers.

3. Passive Analog Signal Filters: RC low-pass and high-pass filters attenuate high-frequency radio frequency noise or eliminate DC offsets in audio processing systems, microphones, and sensor telemetry amplifiers.

4. Rapid High-Current Discharge Energy Storage: Defibrillators, pulsed lasers, and camera strobe flash units charge capacitors progressively through high resistances from a battery, subsequently releasing stored joules across tiny load resistances in microseconds to produce hundreds of amperes safely.

Practice Problems & Diagnostic Tasks

Test your comprehension of electrostatic geometry and RC circuit transients using the five laboratory tasks below:

Task 1: Physical Geometry Calculation

A parallel-plate capacitor is assembled with square plates measuring 20 cm on each side, separated by a 0.5 mm paper dielectric barrier (κ = 3.7). Taking ε0 = 8.854 × 10^-12 F/m, calculate the total capacitance of this component in nanofarads (nF).

Task 2: Time Constant & 1-τ Charging Voltage

A 2200 μF capacitor is connected in series with a 4.7 kΩ resistor across a 12.0 V DC power supply. Calculate the circuit time constant (τ) in seconds, and determine the exact potential difference across the capacitor after charging for one complete time constant (t = τ).

Task 3: Instantaneous Discharge Current

A capacitor charged to 24.0 V discharges through a 150 Ω resistor. What is the initial peak current at t = 0 s? Assuming the circuit time constant is 3.0 seconds, determine the remaining instantaneous loop current at time t = 6.0 seconds.

Task 4: Reverse Calculation for Target Charge Time

An emergency strobe system requires a 10.0 mF capacitor charged from a 15.0 V supply to reach 14.0 V before firing. If the series charging resistor is 500 Ω, how long must the charging cycle run before the strobe can fire?

Task 5: Stored Electrostatic Energy & Average Power

A defibrillator capacitor with C = 32.0 μF is charged to 5000 V. Calculate the total electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor. If 80% of this energy is delivered to a patient's chest during a discharge pulse lasting 4.0 milliseconds, calculate the average electric power delivered during the shock.

Solution to Task 1:

Plate area A = 0.20 m × 0.20 m = 0.04 m² = 400 cm².
Distance d = 0.5 × 10^-3 m = 0.0005 m.
C = κ × ε0 × (A / d) = 3.7 × (8.854 × 10^-12 F/m) × (0.04 m² / 0.0005 m).
C = 3.7 × (8.854 × 10^-12) × 80 = 2.6208 × 10^-9 F = 2.62 nF.

Solution to Task 2:

Time constant τ = R × C = (4700 Ω) × (2200 × 10^-6 F) = 10.34 s.
At t = τ, Vc(τ) = V0 × (1 - exp(-1)) = 12.0 V × (1 - 0.367879) = 12.0 V × 0.63212 = 7.59 V.

Solution to Task 3:

Initial peak current at t = 0 s: I0 = V0 / R = 24.0 V / 150 Ω = 0.160 A (160 mA).
At t = 6.0 s, the elapsed dimensionless time is t / τ = 6.0 s / 3.0 s = 2.0.
Instantaneous current: I(6.0s) = I0 × exp(-2) = 0.160 A × 0.135335 = 0.0217 A (21.7 mA).

Solution to Task 4:

Time constant τ = R × C = 500 Ω × (10.0 × 10^-3 F) = 5.0 s.
From Vc = V0 × [1 - exp(-t / τ)], we set 14.0 = 15.0 × [1 - exp(-t / 5.0)].
14.0 / 15.0 = 0.93333 = 1 - exp(-t / 5.0) ⇒ exp(-t / 5.0) = 0.06667.
Taking natural logarithms: -t / 5.0 = ln(0.06667) ≈ -2.708.
t = 5.0 × 2.708 = 13.54 s.

Solution to Task 5:

Total stored energy U = (1/2) × C × V² = 0.5 × (32.0 × 10^-6 F) × (5000 V)².
U = 0.5 × (3.2 × 10^-5) × (2.5 × 10^7) = 400.0 Joules.
Energy delivered to chest = 0.80 × 400 J = 320.0 Joules.
Pulse duration Δt = 4.0 × 10^-3 s = 0.004 s.
Average Power P = Energy / Δt = 320 J / 0.004 s = 80,000 W (80.0 kW).
