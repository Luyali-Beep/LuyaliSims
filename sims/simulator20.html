How to Operate the Simulator

This interactive laboratory provides three dedicated physical configurations for testing moments and static equilibrium. Select an experimental mode from the top console:

1. Fulcrum Lever: Adjust the masses and positions of Load 1 and Load 2 along the beam. You can shift the central fulcrum pivot away from the center to study the effect of the beam's own self-weight. Observe angular tipping in real time when net torque is non-zero, or click Auto-Bal to compute the precise counter-lever distance.

2. Suspended Beam: Shift dual overhead supporting cables and asymmetric hung loads. Monitor the upward string tensions (T₁ and T₂). If a load moves excessively beyond an anchor, notice how the distant cable slacks (T approaches 0 N) while the loaded cable bears the system's entire downward weight.

3. Tilting Metal Box: Test the overturning stability of a rectangular body. Vary the box mass, base width, push height, and applied horizontal force. Watch how the restoring moment (driven by gravity through the center of mass) competes against the overturning moment (driven by the lateral pushing force) about the bottom-edge pivot.

Use the Hide Methods and Show Answers toggles inside the Mathematical Analysis panel to test your manual calculation skills before revealing step-by-step proofs.

Scientific Theory & Working Principles

For any rigid body to remain in complete static equilibrium, two fundamental conditions of Newtonian mechanics must be fulfilled simultaneously:

First Condition (Translational Equilibrium): The vector sum of all external forces acting upon the system must equal zero. In a vertical 2D plane:

Sum of Upward Forces (ΣF_up) - Sum of Downward Forces (ΣF_down) = 0
R_pivot = W_total = (m_1 + m_2 + m_beam) · g

Second Condition (Rotational Equilibrium): The vector sum of all external moments (or torques) acting about any chosen pivot axis must equal zero. The Principle of Moments dictates that when a body is in rotational equilibrium:

Sum of Anti-Clockwise Moments = Sum of Clockwise Moments
Στ_ACW = Στ_CW
Σ(F_i · d_i) = 0

Where F represents the acting force and d is the perpendicular distance between the line of action of the force and the chosen pivot point.

Center of Gravity & Stability Criteria

The Center of Gravity (C.G.) is the single point through which the entire weight of a body acts. When a body rests on a flat horizontal plane, its weight exerts a restoring moment that tends to keep its base seated. As a lateral force acts upon the body, tipping begins about the extreme bottom edge pivot. The body topples whenever the vertical line of action of its center of gravity falls outside the boundary of its base of support.

Mathematical Foundations & Formulas

The quantitative calculations performed across the three simulator modes rely on standard classical mechanics equations:

Weight Calculation: W = m · g (where g = 9.8 m/s²)

Moment of a Force: τ = F · d_perpendicular

Fulcrum Lever Balance: (m_1 · g · |d_1 - p|) + (m_beam · g · |0 - p|) = m_2 · g · |d_2 - p|

Suspended Beam Cable Tensions:
Taking moments about Left Cable (s_1):
T_2 · (s_2 - s_1) = Σ [W_i · (d_i - s_1)]
T_1 = W_total - T_2

Critical Toppling Push Force: F_crit = (W · [Width / 2]) / Height_push

Real-World Engineering & Industrial Applications

The Principle of Moments is foundational to several branches of mechanical and structural engineering:

1. Tower Cranes & Cantilever Construction: Heavy construction cranes utilize concrete counterweights placed along a rear counter-jib. The massive clockwise moment produced by the counterweight balances the variable anti-clockwise load moment exerted by hoisted cargo, preventing the central mast from suffering catastrophic bending failure.

2. Suspension Bridges & Cable-Stayed Trusses: As vehicular traffic crosses bridges, weight distribution constantly shifts. Civil engineers design dual suspension towers and anchor cables using moment summation equations to ensure cable tension never exceeds tensile yield limits or induces slack.

3. Vehicle Rollover & Heavy Machinery Stability: SUVs, freight trucks, and forklifts are designed with low centers of gravity and wide wheelbases. As a vehicle navigates a curve, lateral centrifugal force acts through the center of gravity. If the overturning moment exceeds the restoring moment provided by the vehicle's half-track width, rollover occurs.

4. Orthopedic Biomechanics & Ergonomics: The human musculoskeletal system operates through class-1, class-2, and class-3 levers. Tendons apply muscular tensile forces over short moment arms across joints (such as the bicep lifting an object in the palm), requiring large internal forces to balance external loads.

Practice Problems & Diagnostic Tasks

Test your quantitative comprehension using the five laboratory exercises below:

Task 1: Basic Lever Equilibrium Calculation

A uniform wooden meter rule of mass 0.20 kg is pivoted exactly at its 0.50 m mark. A mass of 0.60 kg is placed at the 0.10 m mark (distance of 0.40 m from pivot). Calculate the distance from the pivot at which a 0.80 kg mass must be placed on the opposite side to bring the meter rule into horizontal equilibrium. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

Task 2: Off-Center Pivot with Beam Self-Weight

A uniform metallic beam of length 6.0 m has a mass of 12.0 kg. Its center of gravity is at the 3.0 m mark. The pivot is placed at the 2.0 m mark (1.0 m to the left of the beam's center of gravity). A load of 30.0 kg is placed at the left edge (0.0 m mark, which is 2.0 m from the pivot). Calculate the mass of a counterweight that must be placed at the right end (6.0 m mark, which is 4.0 m from the pivot) to achieve rotational equilibrium.

Task 3: Dual-Support Cable Tensions

A light scaffolding plank of length 8.0 m and mass 20.0 kg is supported by two vertical ropes located at its ends (x = 0.0 m and x = 8.0 m). A painter of mass 70.0 kg stands at x = 2.0 m from the left end. Taking g = 9.8 m/s², determine the tension in the left rope (T₁) and the tension in the right rope (T₂).

Task 4: Critical Toppling Push Force Analysis

A heavy industrial metal cabinet of mass 80.0 kg has a rectangular base with a width of 1.2 m and a height of 2.4 m. Its center of gravity is located at its geometric center (0.6 m from each vertical side, 1.2 m from the base). A horizontal push force is applied at a height of 1.8 m from the ground. Determine the minimum horizontal pushing force required to initiate toppling about the bottom front edge.

Task 5: Cable Slack Condition & Boundary Analysis

A horizontal beam of length 10.0 m and mass 40.0 kg is supported by two vertical cables placed at x = 2.0 m and x = 8.0 m. A mobile inspection robot of mass 60.0 kg traverses toward the right end of the beam. Determine how far to the right of the second cable the robot can travel before the left cable becomes completely slack (T₁ = 0 N).

Solution to Task 1:

1. Identify the pivot: Center of the ruler = 0.50 m mark. The ruler's weight acts at the pivot, so its moment arm is 0 m (zero torque).

2. Anti-clockwise moment from Load 1: τ_ACW = m₁ · g · d₁ = 0.60 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 0.40 m = 2.352 N·m.

3. Clockwise moment from Load 2: τ_CW = m₂ · g · d₂ = 0.80 kg × 9.8 m/s² × d₂ = 7.84 · d₂ N·m.

4. Equating moments: 7.84 · d₂ = 2.352 ⇒ d₂ = 2.352 / 7.84 = 0.30 m (or 30 cm) from the pivot (positioned at the 0.80 m mark).

Solution to Task 2:

1. Pivot is at x = 2.0 m. Distance to left load: d₁ = 2.0 m - 0.0 m = 2.0 m (Anti-clockwise).

2. Distance to beam C.G.: d_beam = 3.0 m - 2.0 m = 1.0 m to the right (Clockwise).

3. Distance to counterweight: d₂ = 6.0 m - 2.0 m = 4.0 m to the right (Clockwise).

4. Setting Στ_ACW = Στ_CW (cancelling g throughout):

(30.0 kg × 2.0 m) = (12.0 kg × 1.0 m) + (m₂ × 4.0 m)

60.0 = 12.0 + 4.0 · m₂ ⇒ 4.0 · m₂ = 48.0 ⇒ m₂ = 12.0 kg.

Solution to Task 3:

1. Total downward forces: W_plank = 20.0 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 196.0 N (acting at x = 4.0 m, midpoint). W_painter = 70.0 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 686.0 N (acting at x = 2.0 m). Total W = 882.0 N.

2. Take moments about Left Rope (x = 0.0 m):

Clockwise moments = (686.0 N × 2.0 m) + (196.0 N × 4.0 m) = 1372.0 + 784.0 = 2156.0 N·m.

Anti-clockwise moment from right rope tension = T₂ × 8.0 m.

T₂ = 2156.0 / 8.0 = 269.5 N.

3. Translational equilibrium: T₁ = Total W - T₂ = 882.0 N - 269.5 N = 612.5 N.

Solution to Task 4:

1. Total weight of cabinet: W = 80.0 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 784.0 N.

2. Restoring lever arm about front pivot edge = Half-width = 1.2 m / 2 = 0.6 m.

3. Restoring Moment: τ_restoring = W × 0.6 m = 784.0 N × 0.6 m = 470.4 N·m.

4. Overturning Moment: τ_overturn = F_push × Height_push = F_push × 1.8 m.

5. At the critical tipping point: F_push × 1.8 m = 470.4 N·m ⇒ F_push = 470.4 / 1.8 = 261.33 N.

Solution to Task 5:

1. Let the right cable position (x = 8.0 m) serve as the pivot. Span between cables = 8.0 m - 2.0 m = 6.0 m.

2. Beam C.G. is at x = 5.0 m, which is 3.0 m to the left of the right cable.

3. When T₁ = 0 N (cable slacks), the right cable acts as a knife-edge pivot.

4. Anti-clockwise moment about the right cable: Provided by beam weight = 40.0 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 3.0 m = 1176.0 N·m.

5. Clockwise moment from robot located at distance d_overhang past the right cable: (60.0 kg × 9.8 m/s²) × d_overhang = 588.0 · d_overhang N·m.

6. Setting moments equal: 588.0 · d_overhang = 1176.0 ⇒ d_overhang = 2.0 m (robot reaches x = 10.0 m, the exact right tip of the beam, before tipping begins).
